Q1

What are we buying more of?

Benchtop gadgets. Imports of the cookers category that includes air fryers rose 129% and vacuums 91% since 2015, while TVs (+14%) and washing machines (+13%) barely moved.

Of the nearly 12,000 labelled appliance models on sale, only 21 list Australia as a place of manufacture, so counting what crosses the border is a good way to see what we are buying. Over the last ten years the big things in our homes, such as fridges, washing machines and TVs, grew slowly. The small things on the kitchen bench have taken off.

21labelled models that list Australia as a place of manufacture [D1]

Vacuum cleaners (+91%) and the cookers category that includes air fryers (+129%) grew far faster than Australia's population, which rose 16% over the same ten years. The heatmap below shows the path year by year. The COVID-19 years of 2020–22 stand out as everyone set up home. Many categories cooled off in 2023, then benchtop gadgets climbed again to new records.

+16%Australia's population growth, June 2015 to June 2025 [D4]
Q2

When and where do we spend?

Across Australia, spending peaks in November and December every year. Per person, the Northern Territory spends the most and the ACT the least.

The Australian Bureau of Statistics tracks what households spend each month on "furnishings and household equipment", which covers household appliances and tools as well as furniture, floor coverings and household goods [R7]. Two patterns jump out: when in the year we buy, and how the pandemic hit each state differently.

$2,359spent per Australian on furnishings and household equipment in the 12 months to August 2026 [D3, D4]
Black Friday has caught up with Christmas. In 2019, November was only 13% above an average month while December was 25% above. By 2025 the two months were level, both about 25% above average. The ABS itself credited Black Friday sales for the November 2025 rise, when this category grew more than any other [R6]. February is the quietest month every year.
13% → 25%how far November rose above an average month, 2019 to 2025 [D3]

The pandemic was a home-improvement boom almost everywhere. By September 2020, most states were spending 15–25% more than in 2019. Victoria was the exception. During Melbourne's Stage 4 lockdown, when most shops were shut from 5 August to 28 October 2020 [R8], its spending fell below 2019 levels, then jumped to 43% above in November once stores reopened. Today, people in the Northern Territory, Queensland and South Australia spend the most per head, and Canberrans the least.

+43%Victoria's spending in November 2020 compared with its 2019 average month [D3]
Q3

Where are our appliances made?

Almost all of them in Asia and Europe. China alone supplied about half of the appliances imported in 2025, by value.

Hardly any of our appliances are made here. So where do they come from? Two independent datasets tell the same story: customs records of what is shipped in, and the government register of every model that carries an energy rating label.

52%of 2025 appliance import value with a named origin came from China [D2]

China dominates small appliances, supplying 93% of microwaves and 62% of vacuums by value. Bigger appliances have more varied supply chains. Thailand and Poland supply most clothes dryers, Germany and Czechia supply 39% of dishwashers, Germany and Italy supply 36% of ovens and cookers, and Vietnam and Indonesia supply 44% of our televisions.

93%of microwave ovens imported in 2025, by value, came from China [D2]

Switch between appliances. China makes the most models of every type. European-made dryers, dishwashers, washing machines and TVs carry more stars on average. Fridges are the exception, where Thailand and China match Europe.

69%of clothes dryer models on sale list China as a place of manufacture [D1]
Q4

Which brands and star ratings save power?

Miele, LG, Bosch, Haier, Hisense, Fisher & Paykel and TCL never fall below the typical star rating for any appliance they sell. And stars pay: a typical 10‑star dryer costs about a quarter as much to run as a 1‑star one.

Every fridge, washer, dryer, dishwasher and TV sold in Australia carries an energy rating label. More stars means less electricity compared with models of a similar size and features [R1], and the label also shows the expected kWh (kilowatt-hours, the unit on your power bill) used each year. Do brands differ?

1–10stars on the label; more stars means less power than similar-sized models [R1]
The dryer divide. Clothes dryers fall into two clusters. Every vented dryer on the register earns 2.5 stars or fewer and typically uses about 306 kWh a year. The 7–10 star models are all condenser dryers, typically using about 135 kWh. The government says an efficient heat-pump dryer uses 60% less energy than a conventional one [R3]. Brands in the red mostly sell vented or basic 2–3-star condenser dryers, while the typical LG, Miele or Fisher & Paykel dryer has 9 stars.
−56%typical energy use of a 7–10-star dryer compared with a vented dryer [D1]

For dryers, washing machines and TVs, almost every extra star cuts the bill. A typical 1-star dryer costs about four times as much to run as a typical 10-star dryer. At 35c per kWh, that gap adds up to more than $1,000 over ten years. Fridges are the twist: stars only compare models "of a similar size and features" [R1], so a huge 4-star fridge can still cost more to run than a small 2-star bar fridge.

$1,08910-year power cost gap between a typical 1-star and 10-star dryer at 35c/kWh [D1, R4]
Q5

Does paying more buy a happier owner?

No. The most expensive fridges and washer-dryers had the lowest average ratings of their category.

Australia has no open database of product reviews, so we turn to one of the largest public collections: more than 300,000 Amazon reviews of major appliances, gathered by researchers at UC San Diego. These shoppers are mostly American, but they buy the same kinds of products, including brands sold here such as Samsung, LG, Haier, Midea and Bosch.

308,019reviews of major appliances, up to September 2023 [D5]

Very few people bother to give 3 stars. Owners tend to either love their appliance or be very unhappy with it. Built-in dishwashers are the most divisive: almost half of their reviews are 1-star.

49%of built-in dishwasher reviews give 1 star [D5]

Not really. For fridges and washer-dryers, the most expensive models (outlined) are the lowest rated of their category. Mid-priced appliances often score as well as anything.

2.9 / 5average rating of fridges priced US$1,000 or more, the lowest fridge price band [D5]

Before you buy your next appliance

Count the stars, then check the kWh

Stars compare models of the same size. The kWh figure on the label tells you the real yearly use.

Shop the Black Friday sales

November now rivals December for household equipment spending, and the ABS links the rise to Black Friday discounts.

Expensive ≠ better loved

Premium price tags did not earn higher owner ratings. Spend on stars instead: an efficient dryer can save over $1,000 in power across ten years.
